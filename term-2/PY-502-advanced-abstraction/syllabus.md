# PY-502 — Advanced Abstraction: Protocols, Descriptors, Metaprogramming

**Term:** 2 · **Credits:** 15 · **Nominal hours:** 125
**Prerequisites:** PY-501, SE-511
**Co-requisite:** SE-521

---

## Driving question

> When is metaprogramming a better answer than repetition?

The honest answer is: less often than the people who enjoy metaprogramming believe, and
more often than the people who fear it believe. This course teaches the full toolkit —
protocols, descriptors, class hooks, decorators, generators, context managers, declarative
model construction, and internal DSLs — and then spends its final lesson teaching you to
resist it.

The failure this course prevents: a codebase where a new engineer cannot find where a
method is defined, because it is generated by a metaclass that reads annotations set by a
decorator applied by a registry. Every individual step was clever. The result is
unmaintainable, and the person who built it enjoyed every minute.

## Learning outcomes

On completion you will be able to:

1. **Design** interfaces structurally, choosing correctly between duck typing, `Protocol`,
   and ABC on stated criteria.
2. **Build** descriptors that manage per-instance state correctly under inheritance,
   `__slots__`, pickling, and copying.
3. **Use** `__init_subclass__`, `__set_name__`, and class decorators in preference to
   metaclasses, and state precisely the cases where only a metaclass will do.
4. **Write** decorators that compose, preserve signatures and types, carry state safely,
   and work on functions, methods, classes, and coroutines.
5. **Construct** lazy pipelines with generators, and reason about their memory, exception,
   and cleanup semantics.
6. **Compose** resources with context managers and `ExitStack`, with correct behaviour on
   partial failure.
7. **Choose** between `dataclasses`, `attrs`, `pydantic`, `msgspec`, and hand-written
   classes, and defend the choice on named criteria.
8. **Design** a small internal DSL, and articulate what it costs in tooling support,
   debuggability, and onboarding.
9. **Assess** an abstraction's value: state the cost of indirection and apply a decision
   rule to real code.
