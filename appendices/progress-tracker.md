# Appendix — Progress Tracker

Copy this file, keep it beside your study repository, and fill it in as you go. It exists for three
reasons: to see where you are, to make the honest self-assessment that a self-directed degree
requires, and — most usefully — to notice when you have started skipping the exercises.

**The one rule that makes this work:** mark a lesson complete only when the **Core** exercises are
done. Reading a lesson is not completing it. A tracker full of ticks earned by reading is worse than
no tracker, because it tells you a comfortable lie.

Use `⬜ not started · 🟡 read · 🟢 core done · ⭐ challenge done`.

## Retrieval schedule

Spaced retrieval is the highest-return study habit available, and the one people skip because it is
uncomfortable. Do the self-check questions from memory, without the lesson open.

| Interval | What to review | Done |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Self-checks from the last two lessons | |
| Monthly | Self-checks from the current course, all lessons so far | |
| End of term | Self-checks from both courses; the exam under real conditions | |
| Every 6 months | The twelve-paper core; the glossary, cover to cover | |

## Term-end review

At the end of each term, answer these in your log. They take twenty minutes and they are what turn a
sequence of courses into an education.

1. What can I now do that I could not at the start of this term?
2. Which lesson's exercises did I skip, and why? Is that a gap I should close?
3. What did I get wrong on the exam, and what does the pattern of errors tell me?
4. Which idea from this term have I already used in real work?
5. What is the honest hours-per-week I sustained, and is my plan still realistic?
6. Anything wrong in the material? → `appendices/errata.md`

*A note on the marks. Nobody is checking. The exams are worth taking under real conditions —
three hours, closed book, no machine — precisely because nobody is checking: the only person the
result informs is you, and a result you inflated informs you of nothing. Mark yourself against the
rubric in `00-program/assessment-and-rubrics.md`, and mark yourself harshly. The discomfort of a
low mark on a self-administered exam is the cheapest feedback in the whole programme.*
